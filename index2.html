The big picture   /   Species richness

Where is birdlife most diverse?

Comparing the number of bird species recorded across Australian states and territories.

Context

Each total represents the number of distinct native bird species recorded in a state or territory, rather than individual birds.

At a Glance

Queensland records the highest species richness (642), more than double Tasmania's total (307).

Queensland's remarkable bird diversity is shaped by more than its size. The Wet Tropics occupy less than 0.2% of Australia's land area yet support around 40% of its bird species, demonstrating how biodiversity can be concentrated in rainforest habitats. Meanwhile, Western Australia's coastline includes Roebuck Bay, an internationally important feeding ground for migratory shorebirds.

Tasmania offers a different perspective. Despite its lower overall species richness, its geographic isolation has helped shape 12 endemic bird species found nowhere else on Earth.

Reading the heatmap

Island vs Mainland

Tasmania’s island and marine habitats explains its unusually high proportion of petrels and shearwaters (12.7%), while honeyeaters are prominent across much of mainland Australia.

Beyond Species Counts

Two regions can have similar species totals yet very different bird-family compositions. Looking at relative family shares reveals patterns that overall species richness cannot capture.
